### If you want to gather honey, don't kick over the beehive!

#### Principle 1: Don't criticize, condemn or complain

Humans are not creatures of logic. They follow emotion, prejudice, pride and vanity. They never criticize
themselves no matter how wrong they are, so it is foolish to scold them. Any criticism will put them put them on the
defensive and cause immediate self-justification. Criticism wounds their pride, degrades their feeling of self-
importance, demoralizes them, and arouses resentment. Your criticism will turn back on you as they start to justify
themselves. Remember to always begin on others improvements by reflection on self-improvement.

B.F. Skinner showed that positive classical conditioning is the best way to change an animals behavior. The same
is true in humans. Punishing the negative just does not work as well, nor does authoritative regurgitation of
regulations because both of these approaches produce sullen, temporary acceptance. Friendly, concerned reminders
and the discussion of the true purpose of regulations will result in increased compliance without the resentment or
upset feelings.

It takes true character to control, understand, and forgive. Instead of condemning people, try to figure out the
motive behind an action. It is more profitable and intriguing than criticism and it breeds an atmosphere of sympathy,
tolerance, and kindness. To know all is to forgive all. "God himself does not intend to judge men until the end of
days, why should you or I?

A construction manager was walking to the job and spotted a group of people standing under the building
with their hard hats off. He could have went up and yelled at them and told them to put their hats on because of the
rules. Instead he walked up in a casual manner and said "Gentlemen, I know that these rules can seem a little
stringent sometimes and I had a guy on site get hit in the head by a beam a month ago. I care about you all and don't
want you to get hurt.”

President Lincoln was very impudent and wrote many anonymous letters to the paper criticizing his political
opponents. One of these opponents discovered who was writing them and challenged Lincoln to a duel. Right before
the firing, the seconds stopped the duel because Lincoln apologized. After that he said he would never write an
insulting letter or ridicule anyone again. He took the view concerning others that "Don't criticize them; they are just
what we would be under similar circumstances.” In a challenging situation, pull out a 5 dollar bill and ask how

Lincoln would handle the situation.
Benjamin Franklin's secret to political success was "I will speak ill of no man...and speak all the good of
everyone."

### The Big Secret of Dealing with People

#### Principle 2: Give honest and sincere appreciation

Make people want to do what you want them to. Luckily, all people have a gnawing, unfaltering human
desire to be both appreciated and feel important. Some theories suggest that insanity is actually a coping mechanism
to fill this need for importance. The main reason given by runaway wives is "Lack of Appreciation.”

Charles Schwab was the first employee in America to earn over 1 million dollars per year. He was hired by
J.D. Rockefeller because of his people skills. His secret was "his ability to arouse enthusiasm.” The way he
developed that was appreciation and encouragement. Nothing kills ambition like criticism, so instead of that he
provided incentives. He was anxious to praise and loath to find fault; hearty in approbation and lavish in praise. He
thought "their is not a person, no matter how great or exalted, who didn't put forth greater effort under a spirit of

approval than he would under criticism.
Think of the saying "Once I did bad and that I heard ever; twice I did good, but that I heard never." Alfred

Lunt expanded with "There is nothing I need so much as nourishment for my self-esteem."

Beware! Flattery, which is telling the other person exactly what you think they want to hear or what they
already know, is not a working substitute for appreciation because it is shallow, selfish, and insincere. It is counterfeit
currency and will cause trouble if passed. Think of this as more of a new way of life. Live by the saying "Teach me
neither to proffer nor accept cheap praise.” So stop thinking about yourself and turn your attention to the work that
others do for you; like the chef for the delicious meal he just cooked or the lecturer for the excellent speech. This is
appreciation and forms the legal tender of the soul; people will cherish your words and treasure them years after

you've forgotten them.

Carnegie wrote the following message in his mirror. "I shall pass this way but once; any good, therefore, that
I can do or any kindness that I can show to any human being, let me do it now. Let me not defer or neglect it, for I
shall not pass this way again."

He who can do this has the whole world with him. He who can't walks a lonely way.

#### Principle 3: Arouse in the other person an eager want

Every act that every (rational) person has every performed has been out of self-interest, so expect people to do
what is best for them in any situation. Harry A. Overstreet, in his book "Influencing Human Behavior", said "Actions
spring out of what we desire, so to persuade, first arouse in the person an eager want.! So, always talk in terms of
what the other person wants." Henry Ford said 'H their is any one secret of success, it lies in the ability to get the other
person's Point of View and see from their angle as well as yours,"

Before you even enter any situation where you will attempt to persuade anyone, ask yourself "How can I get
this person to do what I want?". Although you may prefer strawberries, fish prefer worms, so bait your hook to suit
the fish. Talk about what they want and show they can get it through following your idea.

An example is that Dale Carnegie bet his sister 100 dollars that he could get his busy (too busy to write their
mom!) nephews to respond to his correspondence before she could. So, he sent them a letter saying that 5 dollars was
enclosed; Dale "forgot" to enclose the 5 dollars and received 3 prompt replies. He then collected his 100 dollars. He
knew his nephews wanted money so he spoke in their language.

A man got his child to look forward to kindergarten by fingerpainting with his family and saying "Only
people who are learning in school can participate.” The family acted like they were having a great time and the kid
was up before his parent waiting to be taken to school. The father knew his kid wanted a feeling of importance. He
gave it to him after his son accomplished something.

Carnegie held a conference every year at the same place. The hotel suddenly raised the rate by 300%. He
went to the hotel manager and, instead of yelling or arguing, he took out a piece of paper and labeled one half
' Advantages of price increase’ and the other half 'Disadvantages of price increase. On the Advantages side he wrote
immediate increased revenue. On the one side he wrote about how this conference was guaranteed annual bookings,
how it was free advertisement, and the value of a prestigious conference being held at the hotel. The manager dropped
the price increase to 50% without Carnegie even mentioning it. An argument would have hurt the manager's pride
and even if Dale won, he would have not got a decrease.

What is wrong with this negotiation? "I want you to come play ball. I like it, but noone else comes to the
gym. The last time I played I got a black eye. I wish you would all come down tonight because I want to play."
Whose wants did he discuss? He needs more pep, fun for others, and a way to stimulate their appetite.

Self-expression is a dominant necessity of human nature. So when we have a brilliant idea, kick it out and let
others stir it and think it is theirs. Maybe they will like it and eat a couple helpings of it.

Owen D. Young said "People who can put themselves in the place of others and understand how their mind
works, need not worry about what the future has in store for them." Remember that this is not a detriment, each
person gets what they want from negotiations. /

### Do this and you'll be welcome anywhere

#### Principle 1: Become genuinely interested in other people

People notice themselves first in photos. They are interested in themselves first. The reason dogs are so
popular is because they know instinctually to be happy when they see their masters. You can make more friends in
two months by becoming genuinely interested in others than in two years of trying to make them interested in you; so
don't try to impress others with your accomplishments as it won't make true, sincere friends.

To make friends do things that require time, energy, unselfishness, and thoughtfulness; like remembering a
birthday. Greet others with animation and enthusiasm. Say ‘Hello' in a pleased tone that radiates interest both on the
phone and in person. Follow this principle religiously in order to develop real friends, and help others while helping
yourself. A person is interested in you when you are sincerely interested in them.

Alfred Adler, the psychologist who wrote ‘What Life Should Mean to You', said "It is the individual who is
uninterested in his fellow man who has the greatest difficulties in life and provides the greatest injury to others. These
individuals spring forth all human failures.” :

Remember that publishers can tell whether someone likes people or not in his first two paragraphs and people
who don't like stories about others also don't like other people.

Thurston, a famous magician, succeeded because of his ability to put his personality across the footlights. He
liked people and genuinely appreciated his audience. He would repeat "I love my audience and will give them the
best possible” before going on stage. This concern for the individual is what gives great salesmen many sells.
Concern for seemingly unimportant people that have some pull is also extremely important: salesclerk, janitors,
secretaries.

A fuel salesman stopped by a stubborn possible customer's store and discovered what he was passionate
about. He then staged a fake debate in the town taking the customers side.

A man found out an executive's hobby of collecting stamps before a meeting. So, before he started talking
business he casually mentioned stamps and read up on them beforehand. This sparked immediate interest and set a
good tone for the beginning of the meeting that helped the man to sell to the executive.

### A simple way to make a good first impression

#### Principle 2: Smile

Your actions speak louder than your words. So, the expression one wears is just as, if not more, important
than their clothes. A smile conveys the statement "I like you, you make me happy and I am glad to see you." Just
think about why people are happy to see a dog (whose actions say "I like you and am glad to see you") or a smiling
baby. A smile is so powerful that telephone company operators smile while on the phone; the smile is conveyed to ~
the listener.

A husband, after 18 years, woke up and looked in the mirror. He thought "whipe that scowl off that sour puss
of yours today. You are going to smile, starting now." He tried it for a week and said ‘Good Morning Dear' to his
wife at breakfast. His attitude change increased the happiness in himself and those around him. Soon, everyone he
smiled at was smiling back at him. He gave them praise and appreciation when they were used to criticism. Even if
you aren't happy, force yourself to smile and act like you are happy. You will become more cheerful. Control your
thoughts to change your happiness and inner condition. "Their is nothing good or bad but thinking makes it so" -

Shakespeare. "Most folks are as happy as they make up their minds to be." - Lincoln.

We resent a mechanical or insincere grin. It fools nobody. When smiling, think "I like this person.” Think
about the doctor who took a less paying job to be with happier people. Working alone prevents you from making
friends and is lonely. You have to go out and make friends, smiling helps. People rarely succeed at a job unless they
enjoy doing it. You must have a good time meeting people if you expect them to like you.

Elbert Hubbard once said, "Whenever you go out of doors, draw the chin in, carry the crown high, fill the
lungs, drink in the sunshine and greet friends with a smile. Put your soul into hand clasps. Don't fear being
misunderstood and don't waste your time thinking of enemies. Fix your goal firmly in your mind, then move straight
to that goal. Keep your mind set on great things you would like to do. As the days go gliding away, find yourself

high and chin in, we are gods in chrysalis."

Smiling is the messenger of good will and breaks the gloom of 12 frowns.

### An easy way to become a good conversationalist

#### Principle 4: Be a good listener, Encourage others to talk about themselves

People are 100 times more interested in themselves than you; a toothache to them is more interesting and
worthy of consideration than 40 earthquakes in other places...remember that. They are interested in expanding their
egos by talking about their own traits and experiences. Catering to this fact makes you seem like a very good
conversationalist. So listen intently with genuine interest and be hearty in praise and approbation. Giving them a
feeling of importance will counter any imagined or real grievance. A good recipe to be despised is: Never listen,
interrupt often, and talk about only yourself.

When someone explains something to you, tell them their instruction entertains you immensely and that you
wish you had their knowledge and must see them again. "Successful business intercourse is exclusive attention to the
speaker", Dr. Eliot, Harvard President. Freud had concentrated attention on his patients, mild, genial eyes, and low
loud voice in order to gain the trust of his patients.

A man thanked a scathing customer for telling him how bad the company was. He commented that he was
embarrassed by the company and empathized with the customer, he made a long time friend out of him.

Even Lincoln felt easier after talking over the Emancipation Proclamation. All we want during times of
trouble is a friendly, sympathetic listener.

### You can't win an argument

#### Principle 1: The only way to get the best out of an arguement is to avoid it

Why prove to a man that he is wrong? What do you accomplish? If you win, it is definitely not going to
make him like you because you will hurt his pride. Let him save face when he is wrong, especially if he did not ask
your opinion. Always avoid the acute angle because it will embarrass you, him or both. Most arguments end with the
contestants being more firmly convinced of their correctness. If you win, the other person will probably revert back
to their old thoughts; a man convinced against his will is of the same opinion still. Remember, if you win you lose
and if you lose you lose, so it is best to learn to not speak and avoid verbal fights.

Remember the trucking company sales rep who was told that the "Brand-Name" Trucks were better than his.
He turned the tables by agreeing that the other company was very good. If he would have argued it would have put
the other man on the defensive. Instead, he was prepped to listen to the real sales pitch.

Also the man who had a dispute with a tax collector's figures. Instead of arguing he began to discuss how
hard it must be to go around and nobley collect all the taxes. It reconfirmed the collectors importance and good will,
which helped the man to make his case. Lincoln once said 'A man resolved cannot spare time for personal contention
or afford the consequences of a loss of temper or self-control.

The following is from 'Bits and Pieces' by the Economic Press on how to not cause arguments from
disagreements.

- Welcome to a disagreement. Two parties will enter an arguement when both of the members
  are necessary and have conflicting interests and views. Start by being thankful that the other point
  was brought to your attention, perhaps this will be a chance to correct your actions before making a
  serious mistake.

- Distrust your first instinctual impression as it will be defensive. Be careful, stay calm and
  watchful of yourself, and control your temper; you can measure people by what makes them angry.

- Listen first and let them finish completely. Don't resist, defend or debate because it will raise
  barriers of misunderstanding instead of build bridges of understanding.

- Look for areas of agreement and concede those after consideration.

- Be honest and admit errors apologetically in areas you need improvement. This will disarm
  and take the person off the defensive.

- Promise to think over your opponents ideas and study them carefully. Always keep in mind
  that anyone who will spend time disagreeing may be right and sincerely wants you to see that, so be
  honest here. If you fail after not considering their point, they can say ‘we tried to tell you, but you
  wouldn't listen.

- Thank your opponent sincerely for their interest and concern.

- Postpone action to give yourself time to think things out thoroughly. Suggest a new meeting
  the next day. Prepare for this meeting by asking yourself:

  Could they be right or partly right? Is their merit or truth to their side? Will my reaction
  relieve the problem or push them away? Will my reaction elevate good peoples estimation of me?
  Will I win or lose? What price will victory cost? Will this blow over if I just leave it alone? Is this
  difficulty an opportunity to me? Listen when they scream.

#### Principle 2: Show respect for others opinions. Never say "You're wrong’

People are wrong all the time. President Roosevelt thought that if could be right 75% of the time he would
reach the highest possible measure of his expectations. If you are right on Wall Street 51% of the time you will be a
millionaire.

Never begin a conversation with 'I will show you that you are wrong' because it will be interpreted as 'I am
smarter that you and I will change your ignorant mind." Words, looks, and intonations can all convey the same thing,
it all results in a direct blow to their feelings, intelligence, judgment, pride, and self-respect. Alexander Pope once
said, "Men must be taught as if you taught them not and things unknown as things forgot." Galileo 'You can't teach
men, only lead him to find it in himself.! Lord Chesterfield to son ‘Be wiser than other people if you can, but don't tell
them so.' Socrates 'One thing only I know and that is that I know nothing.’

So, don't hamstring yourself by challenging the person you are convincing. Instead, try these, 'I thought
otherwise, but I may be wrong. I frequently am and if I'm wrong I want to be put right. So let's examine the facts.’
A car dealer handled all complaints by saying that his dealership could be wrong and that they "made so many
mistakes that he was frequently ashamed. We may have erred, so let's examine the facts." This approach disarms the
other person with a show of respect and courtesy. You will never get into trouble by admitting you could be wrong, it
stops the argument.

James Harvey Robinson wrote ‘The Mind in the Making'. He said that we change our minds often and
without emotion or resistance, but when we are told we are wrong we resent and harden our hearts. We are heedless
in forming beliefs, but passionate when someone attempts to rob us of their companionship. The ideas are not what is
actually dear; it is our self-esteem that is threatened. 'My' is the most important word in human affairs and dealing
with it properly is the beginning of wisdom. We resent that my (car, dog, watch, god, thing) is or could be bad. We
like to continue believing what we have already accepted as the truth. Resentment caused by contradiction causes the
opponent to seek any manner to cling to their ideas or things. The result is the person reasoning’ to find any
argument to defend it with.

Carl Rogers wrote ‘On Becoming a Person’. He would say he found it of enormous value when he could
permit himself to understand the other person. Our first reaction to most statements by others is evaluation or
judgment rather than understanding. Our tendency toward expressions of attitudes, feelings or beliefs is to judge. We
immediately say or feel that the idea or thing is right, wrong, abnormal, nice, stupid, unreasonable and so on. Very
rarely do we permit ourselves to understand precisely what the meaning of the statement is to the other person. Think
about when a friend criticizes something of yours (drapes for instance). You defend whatever it is regardless of if you
don't like them yourself. If the friend would have handled you more gently and tactfully, you may have admitted that
the drapes were terrible and then been proud of your ability io be frank with yourself.

A teacher once told Ben Franklin 'you are impossible. Your opinions slap your opponents and are so
offensive that nobody cares for them. Your friends enjoy themselves more in your absence. You know so much that
no man can tell you anything. Indeed, no one will try because it will only lead to work and discomfort. So you are
not likely ever to know any more than you do now, which is very little. :

He realized that he had to change. He immediately and strictly followed the rule to forbear all direct
