One of the base concept of Objectivism is "Existence is Identity", which can be restated as:

> Existence Identity Consciousness "There is something that I am aware of’

This statement means that we have perceived a thing with our senses (some entity) and have Identified it through the automatic processes our Consciousness. These three concepts (existence, identification and consciousness) represent the basic axioms (self-evident knowledge) on which all of our thoughts, ideas and goals are formed. ### 3.1: Differentiation and Integration as the Means to a Unit-Perspective

There are three steps to identifying an existent awareness (discriminating among senses to perceiving an object), distinguishing (integrating objects in order to identify a unit) and, the one we have not discussed yet, relationships (unit-integration to form abstract concepts). A unit is a method of classification that groups similar things together as they are viewed by the consciousness in a certain existing relationship. A relationship has two parts: differentiation (taking things apart, analysis) and integration (putting things together, synthesis). Once something is formed into a concept, language makes the final concept complete by converting it into a single or a few words. Definition provides an Identity that is definite, understandable and communicable.

### 3.2: Concept Formation as a Mathematical Process

All entities (units) and their actions are measured by attributes in appropriate units within the range of human perception (i.e. length = feet, weight = pounds, etc.,). These perceivable measurements allow us to understand and integrate universal knowledge (i.e. the moon is 240,000 miles away) with our limited perceptive experience (the mile).

Measurements and conceptualization have the same essential purpose, which is to quantitatively identify relationships. They differ in that a concept retains it's characteristics but omits its measurements. This means that the concept has measurements but they are not specified. Omitting a concepts measurements is what actually allows concepts to be formed. Otherwise, if you had only seen oak trees that are 30 feet high your whole life, a new oak tree that is 50 feet high would be an entirely different concept, although it is still an oak tree. Similar concepts thus denote partial identity and partial difference and the relationship among similars possess similar characteristics but in a different measurement or degree.

The first step of conceptualization is the choice to focus on an attribute perceptually, not conceptually, and by a Conceptual Common Denominator (CCD). A CCD is a characteristic reducible to a unit, which differentiates two or more existents from other existents possessing it (i.e. skyscrapers are different from building because of their height). Two or more units possessing the same distinguishing characters with their particular measurements omitted form a Concept Measurement-omission, which recognizes that the measurements do exist in some quantity, is the only way to validate the formation of concepts based on reason.

Concepts denote facts, within the context of the observers knowledge, and cannot be arbitrary since they are based on reality and include all known facts known or perceived. The basic principle of concept-formation is the equivalent of algebra (2x = x + x) in that the quantity 'x' exists in some value, but may be any given value. Conceptual awareness is the algebra of cognition. Therefore, math is the substance of thought and provides the base for induction.

### 3.3: Concepts of Consciousness as Involving Measurement-Omission

First-level concepts are formed directly from perceptual data (cloud, table, etc.,) Higher-level concepts are formed by integrating multiple first-level concepts (e.g. Dalmatians, Bulldogs and German Shepherds are dogs: dogs, fish and cats are pets). One cannot grasp a higher-level concept without first conceptualizing all the lower concepts and percepts. This process rises as high as the person is willing to think. Concepts, therefore, differ from each other by their referents and distance from the perceptual level.

A Concept of Consciousness (CoC) are non-existential (exists only in the mind) and include such concepts as love and thought. A CoC has two primary attributes: Content (the object you are somehow aware of) and Action (the means to which you access the content such as thinking, remembering or imagining). A CoC is the mental integration of two or more instances of a psychological process possessing the same distinguishing characteristics, with the particular contents and measurements of the intensity omitted.

An example is that the measurement-omission of the concept 'thought' pertains to content (i.e. thought is thought regardless of its content) and to intensity (it’s scope, clarity, cognitive and motivational context, mental effort required, etc.,)

### 3.4: Definition as the Final Step in Concept-Formation

Definitions and concepts are not the same thing. Think of a concept as the file folder that contains the information and the definition as the label that identifies and condenses the folder’s contents into one or a few words. The folder is not restricted to its current contents. The folder exists so we can separate out a single unit and then study and interrelate all the data ever to pertain to a given subject Similarly, the definition is not restricted, but is generally more concrete than the folders contents. For example, you may discover that the ’Green Weed’ that has been growing in your front lawn is actually a ’Fern’.

Definition is essential to every concept except axiomatic concepts and concept denoting sensations because the perceptual is already related to reality. A concept, being an integrated floating abstraction, needs a definition that specifies essential characteristics in order to distinguish it from other abstractions. To form a proper definition we must first isolate its units by grasping a distinguishing characteristic (differentia), then on basis of a wider Common Concept Denominator (which are the characteristics reducible to a unit of measurement) give rise to genus. An example of distinguishing man from all other animals would be the genus 'rational'. Another further rule for definitions is of fundamentally, which means that it is the characteristic responsible for the greatest number of distinctive characteristics. A good example is that man could not be distinguished by possessing opposable thumbs because other primates do.

Sometimes new information gives light to a new definition (like the 'Green Weed' being a 'Fern'). This new definition does not contradict the old one because definitions are empirically made in the context of reality and what is known at the time. Every definition implies the beginning 'After full consideration of pertaining facts to this group of existents, the following are the essential, defining characteristics. Similarly, concepts, once formed, do not contradict themselves when reformed or rearranged.

Not following fundamentally leads to the cognitive disaster of confusion, falsehood and distortion. An example of this is grouping the sun, cigars and Jesus together because of the attribute 'encirclement' (the sun and cigars are round and Jesus has a halo). This is bad because the encirclement is not fundamental or causally significant. Similarly bad is defining a valid concept in terms of nonfundamentals. This ultimately obliterates data because the truth or falsehood of all man’s conclusions and thoughts rests on their proper definitions. Concepts designates an existent and all its related characteristics. Concepts are not interchangeable with definitions. For example, man does not mean animal plus rational.

Varying definitions of a concept in vaiying contexts are possible only because the concept means its units, not its definition. A concept is not restricted to its defining characteristics or known characteristics. A concept is an integration of units, which are static regardless of one’s knowledge and are therefore an open-ended classification which includes all of the yet to be discovered characteristics of a given group. This allows research, learning, education and the accumulation and expansion of knowledge.

### 3.5: Concepts as Devices to Achieve Unit-Economy

Perception is limited to a set, small number. Most people can instantly distinguish up to 7 things in a group before it become the vague amount 'many'. For instance you can easily see the number of hash marks in 'HIP but a much larger amount like'///////////////////////////’ is not immediately distinguishable, they must be counted. Emotions are a byproduct of conclusions (concepts) which are formed by integrating previous perceptual data Correcting such a problem requires focus on the lofty task of reintegrating rational ideas about the situation.

### 5.2: Reason as Man's Only Means of Knowledge

A is A. Reason is volitional and our faculty of awareness. A conflict between thought (reason) and emotions is a contradiction between current conclusions and the subconscious. Thus, it is dangerous to misuse the mind (be illogical) because it will cause out of context emotions and confusion in future situations.	  ,	.	.

Emotions are not our tools of cognition. Abstractions like intuition and revelation are invalid concepts because emotion (the effect) should never usurp reason (the cause). Any contradictions should be identified at their base and compared to new ideas, under Rational Analysis, and result in a non-contradictory integration. Following reason is the only way a man can live a life in harmony with reality. Reason first emotion consequence is the rule to follow. An honest, rational man does not desire until he has identified and validated the object of desire (which is an emotion).

### 5.3: The Arbitrary as Neither True nor False

Arbitrary claims are those based on either emotion or not in reality. There is no evidence in these situations, so they are normally accompanied with a brazen assertion ('You can't disprove that their are no goblins’). Consequently, they are automatically invalidated. Any idea cut loose from cognition (the arbitrary) cannot be brought into a relationship with reality (trying to disprove it).

Arbitrary is worse than false because it hasn't even been cognated. It provides chaos. Don't argue against arbitrary claims because the speaker will insulate it from logic via brazen claims. A good rule to follow is to require the adduction of evidence to support any claim. Remember that you can't prove nonexistence of an

entity for which there is no evidence (goblins). Also, you can infer the falsehood of not A if A. Refuse to attempt the task of invalidating the arbitrary.

Saying 'I don’t know' in this situation means that the claim has a relationship with cognition (reality) and deserves consideration. 'I don't know' is for when one can't decide on which one of two or more seemingly logical conclusion is warranted. Evaluating the arbitrary obliterates the distinction that it has no place in cognition and is ground in emotion. For instance, agnostics take the stand that their is no god. This stand infers that they have considered the evidence for him (for which their is none outside of 'faith') and concluded he does not exists. Objectivists believe that this view is flawed because it considers the arbitrary as possible. Logic is man's only way of knowing. Below is three steps to take to know whether something is worth consideration:

1. Establish whether the question has a place in relation to the realm of evidence and deserves consideration.
2. Weight the evidence.
3. Make up your mind and take a stand.

### 5.4: Certainty as Contextual

Human knowledge is limited, so our conclusions are based on our specific cognitive context. To validate an idea, reduce it back to axioms or perception (i.e. ground ideas in reality) and evaluate logically. Once you arrive at a reasonable, logical conclusion, the idea is validated. The identify of the cognitive context of a conclusion is 'In accordance with the evidence, and the facts none so far, the following is a proper conclusion to draw.’ Follow up a new idea by continuing to observe and, when new information warrants it, draw better conclusions based on new evidence.

New knowledge in this system is not contradicted, it is built upon. A good example is how people used to think that the universe revolved around the Earth because of their observation that the stars and sun moved circularly across the sky. Later, people discovered that this occurred due to rotation. The first group made the best conclusion with the information they had and later built on it that it was actually rotation. If knowledge is formed logically, the only way it can be contradicted is by context-dropping. Ideas are thus considered to be contextually absolute. This relationship between known and unknown knowledge allows us to say 'If everything points to X, within the conditions, then the conclusion is unshakable.'

Ideas have several states. Possible - Some, but not much, evidence and no contradiction. Probable - The burden of a substantial body of evidence, still inconclusive, supports it This state is relatively extensive, yet still objective grounds to remain in doubt about the final verdict. Certain - The total of evidence points in a single direction. There is nothing to suggest even the possibility of another interpretation. Non arbitrary doubt is a transitional state before a conclusion.

Remember that certainty is a contextual assessment. After a logically formed conclusion you should be able to say to a skeptic 'can you point to any sign of fallacy, such as a logical flaw, neglected fact or improperly defined term in my conclusion' and mean that you are certain of your logical conclusion and have considered all possible alternatives you could think of.

### 6.1: Living Organisms as Goal-Directed and Conditional

Living Organisms know that they can die and this fact is what distinguishes us from inanimate objects.

Our existence is conditional on our self-generated actions in order to achieve an end (survival and its derivatives, food, water, happiness, etc.,). All components of our body have a definite function, we are not indifferent to consequences and want to both maintain and sustain life. Materialists ignore these and believe that all things should and can be reduced to physics; this approach ignores and discredits the role of consciousness in a man's life.

### 6.2: Reason as Man’s Basic Means of Survival

Living Organisms are grouped hierarchically by what they need (their means) in order to survive. The lowest level acts on instinct, sense and stimuli. This group includes plants and jellyfish. The next level acts on pleasure and pain and include animals. Man, the highest, acts by reason. A higher level organism cannot survive via the means of lesser levels. Man cannot survive on lesser means because we must create our own goods (tractors do not grow on trees). Reason is our practical attribute that allows us to produce the goods that help us to survive.

Man has two primary attributes, Matter (body) and Consciousness (soul). A body without a mind is a corpse and a soul without a body is a ghost Complete mind-body integration requires one accept the Primacy of Existence over the Primacy of Consciousness; which means that one believes that reality exists in a definite form and is the primary thing' we must adhere to in order to survive. The opposite point of view is that our consciousness defines reality, so what we think is real becomes real. Everything that man has is a product of the reasoning, integrated mind.

### 6.3: Reason as an Attribute of the Individual

There is no collective mind/brain. Every step forward by a group or civilization is because a single man thought of it. It is his achievement because thought is private. We learn from others, but new ideas are ours (if it is not parroting). Thus the intrinsic statement 'Accept it on faith (because it just is)' is not viable. Collective thought is like a Taggart Transcontinental meeting (no convincing, self-assertion, only emptiness just like the primacy of consciousness it rests on).

A sovereign person is one who holds reason in the highest esteem and chooses to think. He is not controlled by his emotions. He controls his conclusion and has self-made knowledge. Each man could be the master of his own destiny and understand his emotions via choosing his own ends (i.e. taking the time to think long term or to understand/discover the true cause of emotions).

The 'Nature versus Nurture' argument is void to a rational being. Reason, and our degree of accepting it as our guiding principle, dictates how much we affect our own destiny. The power of choice rules man; he is free and the product of his specific exercise of freedom (intellect). Your choices earn you respect, we can all make ourselves heroes.

Ethics and morality are codes of values that guide mans life in dealing with the three basic questions:

### 7.1: Life as the Essential Root of Value

In order to know the key to acting ethical, one must know the ultimate eamable value by which we need to judge and select all other lesser derivative values against. We must select a value because morality is not a primary, it must be reached and defined on the basis of reality (observations).

A value is what one acts to gain or keep (i.e. the object of an action, like food from hunting). So the concept of value infers 'Value to whom?’, 'For what reason' and 'What are the alternatives?' have been reasonably answered by a living organism (subject to live versus death decisions) capable of goal-oriented action. Where there are no alternatives, there can be no goals or values.

The fundamental choice of life or death (existence or non-existence) gives context to all actions and is the precondition of all values. Only a being capable of being destroyed and can prevent it has the needs and values required to exist. So, the ultimate value is life itself. Being alive is the primary value toward which all our decisions should be directed.

A good example of these points is the immortal robot. Such a robot does not have the alternative of life or death and requires no action to sustain itself. So what values can it act for? (Think of Melvin from "The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy") You can not tell it to enjoy a good meal because it has no need of nutritive action. Removing the alternative of life or death also removes the possibility of need satisfaction or need frustration, at least on the physical level, and pleasure or pain. Another good illustration of this point is the suicide of older vampires in "Interview with the Vampire".

Existence exists is the fundamental starting point in philosophy. One cannot debate about why one should prefer to exist because the debate exists here, so deal with it. Since the life versus death alternative creates the context for value-oriented action, any code of values must hold life as the ultimate value. Therefore, what furthers life is good and what destroys it is evil.

Now, a few tidbit of information before we move on. Happiness is the emotion one receives from achieving a value, feeling presuppose value judgments and friends are men who share the same values.

### 7.2: Man's Life as the Standard of Moral Value

Man does not have an inbuilt standard of value or follow an automatic coarse of action (specifically, he does not automatically pursue self-preservation). So, in order to survive, he must choose to hold life as his greatest value and learn to sustain it by discovering the necessary values (that which one acts to gain or keep, object of an action) and virtues (the actions to keep or gain a value).

Morality is a code (integrated hierarchy of uncontradictory, fundamental truths) of values accepted by choice in order to survive. This code shapes our character and course of life.

The exact purpose of moral values is objective. We must act long-range (oriented towards the more distant future) by planning a course of action, modifying our behavior and integrating all of our goals, desires and activities. This process is the only way to achieve a consistent, ultimate purpose.

All actions that living organisms make are either in accordance with what self-preservation requires or not because all of our choices either directly or indirectly affect our ability to survive. Actions against self-preservation do not have to be immediately fatal (like alcoholism), but taking only a little damage is like taking a little cyanide. It is ultimately not good. Life does not mean flirting with death.

Man's life should be assessed as a continuous whole as either good or evil because, throughout our life, we have (or should have) assessed eveiy proximate and remote consequences of our decisions. In other words, our life has been the product of our decisions and can (should) therefore be judged.

Ultimately, our decisions (work, giving, cheating, etc.,) are made by application of our preformed moral concepts. So, to succeed throughout our life we must pick a course of action, goals and values in the context of an entire lifetime. To accomplish a choice that requires analyzing this much information, we must have solid objectively-formed concepts. Survival ultimately occurs at one price, Reason.

### 7.3: Rationality as the Primary Virtue

The basic moral principle tells us that we must hold three things as the supreme ruling values in life: reason (our tool of knowledge), purpose (choice of happiness which the tool must proceed to achieve) and selfesteem (inviolate certainty that the mind is competent to think and is worth of happiness).

Rationality is the act of recognizing, accepting and applying of reason as one’s only source of knowledge, judge of values and guide to action. This also includes choosing and validating of one's opinions, decisions, work, love, etc., in accordance with logic, objectivity and integration. Commitment to rational thought results in a sustained growth in knowledge. It requires effort, struggle and the need of continual, scrupulous, lifelong mental work. Rationality requires us to know our motives (means to end). If you want something, it is your duty to understand why and plot a coarse of action to acquire it if your decide it is a logical motive.

Never put anything over the perception of reality. It is the primary obligation. The primary vice is irrationality (evasion, whim-worship, mystics). An evader, who does not follow logic in the first place, will not follow their evasion logically because they do not integrate their decisions, so their knowledge becomes localized and contradictory. Both unearned effects without causes and whim-worship (wanting something without knowing the reason why) reverse rationality. A good example is the drug addict who lives in the now, ignoring that what he is doing is killing him. Another is the person who want to be loved who does not possess any virtues that deserve to be loved or admired. Both place '1 wish' > 'It is'. Reason should always come first, then emotion.

### 7.4: The Individual as the Proper Beneficiary of His Own Moral Actions

The person who ought to benefit from a man's actions distinguishes the standards of ethics from the purpose of ethics. A standard is an abstract principle that measures or gauges man’s choices in the achievement of a specific purpose.

Egoism is the pursuit of self-interest (selfishness), it states that one ought to benefit from his own actions. It is a corollary of man’s life as the moral standard; if every action we take either furthers life or destruction and we choose what actions we take, then we can either decide to act towards the ultimate goal (self-preservation) or not. Egoism is a moral term because man gets to decide whether or not to embrace self-preservation a fundamental rule of voluntary behavior.

A cognitive sovereign cannot be a moral serf. Rationality requires that the mind be the means to goals in accordance with reasonable judgment of facts and values. No authority is higher and no value greater than facts. Objectivity requires selfishness (rational self interest) inside the context of reason, noncontradictory goals, long- range thought, principled actions and acceptance of causality.

Selfishness (egoism) is not doing whatever you feel like or requiring others to sacrifice for you (men should be able to pursue their own desires). It is doing what rationally benefits you the most in an unforced situation. This is why their can be no conflicts of interest among rational men who live by the concepts of production, trade, accept responsibility of earning values and refuse to make or accept sacrifice.

Social existence is an asset to men because of the amount of trade and knowledge to be gained.

However, ensure that you properly identify the roles of others in your life and evaluate them properly by validating and defining specific values of character that are important to their personality. All intimacy is selfish in nature, furthers self-preservation (happiness) and demands self-esteem (to feel you deserve the relationship). Any assertion of 'selfless love' translates to 'I am indifferent to my values’. In order to say 'I love you [and mean it properly] one must first learn how to say I'. The good is the man sustaining his life by reason, with all the implications of this goal.

It is a relationship between consciousness and existence. An example of a realistic, necessary way of looking at things is: You must eat to survive, work to eat, think to work, look at reality to work, and know your goals and how to achieve them in order to begin looking into reality.

Self-sacrifice for society. Consciousness creates its own reality and is what causes true selfishness that destroys other men.

### 8.3: Honesty as the Rejection of Unreality

Honesty is the refusal to fake reality, i.e. to pretend that A is B. Honesty requires that one face the whole truth on every issue. This is a corollary of rationality, which requires a constant state of full focus, commitment to reality and acknowledgment that existence exists. Honesty is the obverse; the rejection of unreality and the belief that ONLY existence exists. It is the process of taking cognition seriously and rejecting intellectual pretenses of method, motive or content; it is to know what you know, constantly expand knowledge and never evade of fail to correct a contradiction.

The honest man does not seek to obtain any value by deception, to himself or others. He recognizes that the unreal can have no value, be it love, fame, cash, etc.,. He recognizes that value is objective and an evaluation in reference to man’s standard (which is life) that is derived from reality. Value is thus a form of truth, and identity, that must correspond with reality. The satisfaction of desire is not necessarily a value and may conflict with reality. He recognizes that since man lives in reality, he must conform to it. He is guided by action and refuses to fake anything in his own mind. He does not fake science by pretending that emotions prove truth, nor self-esteem by pretending that approval from others proves value. He is not mediocrity eaten by envy feigning greatness or a genius hungry for popularity who feigns mediocrity. He becomes a priest of truth in every aspect of mind, life and soul.

Those who don’t follow honesty, in every form, make reality his enemy. The first step of dishonesty leads to the next. He weaves a new existence. By faking existence he creates a dichotomy between fact (that which he is trying to erase) and desire as he places the value (the objective form of truth) above that which exists. This policy ends in the spread of evil throughout the dishonest mans mind and life. This hypocrite fakes interest in ideas, usually through role-playing, to impress others.

People become more real to him than the fragments of reality he still recognizes. He is a fool whose source of values is the fools he succeeds in fooling.

Is he a man of integrity? His method of action is eschewing moral principles and trying to get away with the fraud of the moment.

Since they inverted the moral code, they were unable to project a convincing argument here. The assertive villains normally stole the fire and drama. Thus, the school retreated from characterization (motivation, psychology, moral values) into costume dramas and fantasy.

Objectivism allows the projection of a fully convincing hero. Ayn Rand presents here characters in what she calls Romantic Realism as how they ought to be. Without integration men can create plots and write beautifully and successfully, but only for a while. If they do not know the deepest meaning and justification of their noble endeavor, they are not in intellectual control of it and the ignoble soon take over. The fall of Romanticism is an eloquent case in point.

### 12.3: Esthetic Value as Objective

The fact that one agrees or disagrees with an artists philosophy or whether it is logical or not is irrelevant to the independent esthetic appraisal of work qua art. A false philosophy can be embodied in a great work of art.

Art is not judged by feelings. The task of the art may be to concretize emotion, but that doesn’t mean that emotion is the tool of cognition. A sense of life is a source of art, but not the means of esthetic judgment There can be no concrete authoritarian religious like commandments on how to judge (which are generally derived from esteemed previous works). The proper review requires a process of reason.

Proper evaluation requires identifying the theme, the abstract meaning of the work, evaluate the means by which he conveys it and finally take his criterion and evaluate the level of technical mastery with which he projects his view of life. To translate a metaphysical feeling one must know both what one wants to express and how to do it. Creativity requires focus, purpose and thought. He can objectify his viewpoint only by adhering to certain rational principles that apply universally. There are three esthetic principles advocated by Ayn Rand (selectivity, clarity and integration).

1. Selectivity in regard to subject. Art, by it's nature, conveys an artists metaphysics and must have a subject. One need not agree, but the concretes chosen by the artist convey an idea believed to be the best suited for a purpose. Since the medium used by an artist is limited, it is selective by nature. The artist must make a conscious rational choice on his subject's characters and events. Since the artist has a definite perspective on reality to convey, he can not properly choose the random or whatever others have chosen.

The primary cause of bad art is imitation. The imitator picks up elements from other works and splashes them together. It is thus devoid of meaning and therefore esthetic value. A similar viewpoint is the artist who refuses to select and offers uncritical slices of life. Since the work does convey a meaning to the observer, it is generally tangential and inconsistent as the metaphysical projection conflicts with the artist's theory and intention.

It contradicts the nature of art and undercuts the artist. Others shrug selectivity because they believe art is only about style. This is an inversion of reality because the subject is the fundamental attribute of art to which all others are the means. The goddess portrayed by the amateur is merely incompetent, whereas the slob portrayed by the technical genius is criminal. The end does not justify the means nor do the means justify the ends. Art is for man’s sake, for the vision of reality it offers.

The extreme of the antisubject attitude is that their should be no recognizable entities or subjects. This amounts to 'the only way to recreate reality is to dispense with it and all representations and conclusions. This is similar to the court emptying the term ’freedom' of substantive meaning while focusing instead on procedural questions such as the ’due process' necessary to deny to some poor soul his inalienable rights. This approach is best represented by the expert Nazi surgeons removing perfectly healthy limbs and organs from the enslaved. Does this not elevate technique above content?

That which is not worth contemplating in life is not worth recreating in art. Misery, disease and evil are proper objects of study in life to understand and correct, but are not proper of contemplation for contemplation’s sake. In art, the negative is only implanted as contrast to stress the positive. One should enjoy thinking about the good and know that evil is impotent. Men may depict heroes, average men or the depraved and end up with good art, so long as, within his context, he adheres to all the principles of good art, selectivity included.

2. Clarity is the quality of being distinct, sharp, evident and fully intelligible to the mind as opposed to mystics or skeptics who want ambiguity, obscurity and confusion. Artists can present any incomprehensible subject (a vast bureaucracy) so long as the presentation is intelligible. The purpose of art is to overcome ambiguity and reveal true existence. A man in full focus will create and respond to a style of radiant clarity and precision, where A is A and everything is open to man and demands its constant functioning. A mystic lost in the fog of his feelings will be moved by the mysterious, where things float, outlines dissolve and A is any non-A.

Art satisfies a need of man's cognitive faculty, so art must conform to cognition’s requirements. They must use all of epistemology: concepts, logic, definition, objects and concretes. It’s meaning is independent of the claims of any interpreter (artist included). It is not necessarily good but it is graspable by a rational being. Nonobjective art deliberately flouts these rules of the human mind creates art that is the equivalent of what the arbitrary is to cognition It is addressed to man as he does not perceive and cannot think. It is defiantly senseless.

3. Integration. The selectively chosen, clearly presented elements of good, objective art must be attributes of an indivisible whole. The whole implies the parts, just as the parts imply one another and the whole. A proper artwork must have all the organic unity of a living creature. Nothing in good art is accidental. Anything insignificant that is included produces a lethal contradiction because art, by it's nature, must mean something but it doesn’t in 'this' case. This contradiction destroys the spell (integrity and power of stylization) and works to make a new reality unreal.

For example, if a good novelist sends his characters to the country for a weekend, he does not interrupt the action to offer a needless description, however loving, of the countryside. If he offers it, he needs it, and an intelligent reader can know why. Similarly, think about how Gail Wynand rose while the accused (Roark) was asked to face the jury. It added intensity but also showed Wynand acknowledging that his life was also on trial. This is nonaccidental detail and magnificent simplicity (economy of art).

Art can be judged rationally. People do not 'just know' nor is it 'just a matter of taste'. These represent the intrinsic versus subjective view. As in ethics it is in esthetics: value is an aspect of reality in relation to man and means the evaluation of a fact. One reduces esthetic principles to the nature of art, the needs of human life and the primary of ethics, which ultimately reduces to the axiom of existence. Beauty is not in the object or the eye of the beholder, it is objective, in the object, as judged by a rational beholder.

Since art is a philosophic composite, it is not a contradiction to say 'this is a great work of art, but I don't like it' provided one defines the exact meaning. The first part is a esthetic appraisal and the second to a deeper philosophical level. It is by the standards of this deeper level, truth and mastery, in the hands of the top practitioners that produce the greatest achievements in art history.
